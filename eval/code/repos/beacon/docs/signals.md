# Light characteristics

What the light and the horn show to a passing vessel, and how long the batteries keep them going.

## Flash pattern

The light shows two white flashes every ten seconds, written Fl(2) W 10s on the chart. Each flash
lasts half a second, and the two flashes of a group are a second and a half apart.

The lens turns once per period, so a lens that turns slower changes the character of the light.
The agent raises an alarm when the lens has not turned for three periods.

## Fog signal

In fog the horn sounds one blast of three seconds every thirty seconds, for as long as the
visibility stays under two nautical miles.

To keep the horn from switching on and off when the visibility hovers around the limit, it stops
only when the visibility rises above two and a half nautical miles.

## Battery reserve

The batteries carry the light for five nights without charging. The solar panel recharges them on
a clear day; a grey week in winter can drain them.

Below forty per cent the agent raises a warning, below twenty per cent an alarm, and the shore
station sends a boat with spare batteries.
