# Harbour operations

How the harbour is run around the scheduler: the people, the handovers and what to do when
something goes wrong.

## Night shift handover

The outgoing harbour master hands over the berth plan, the open work permits and every vessel
waiting outside the breakwater. Vessels expected before the next high water are named one by
one, with their draught and the berth planned for them.

Both masters sign the handover log before the outgoing one leaves the tower. A handover is never
done over the radio.

## Storm protocol

When the forecast wind reaches force 8 on the Beaufort scale, the harbour closes to arrivals.
Vessels at berth double their mooring lines and keep a watch on board. Departures continue only
with the consent of the harbour master.

The scheduler marks the harbour closed by itself from the forecast, and arrivals planned in a
closed hour move to the first open window after it.

## Pilot boarding

Every vessel over 80 metres takes a pilot. The pilot boards at the outer buoy, one hour before
high water, so that the vessel enters on the rising tide with water to spare.

A pilot is never sent out in a swell of more than two metres. A vessel that cannot take a pilot
waits at anchor in the roads.

## Fuel spills

Stop the transfer, close the scuppers and call the duty officer. The booms are stored in the shed
next to berth 3; two people can lay them around a vessel in a quarter of an hour.

Record the time, the berth and the estimated volume in the incident log. The vessel does not
leave until the duty officer has signed the log.
